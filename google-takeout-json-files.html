What are all those .json files in my Google Takeout?

Short answer: they hold your photos' dates, places, captions and more. Do not delete them until the information is written into the photos.

Download for Mac

What they are

For each photo or video, Google adds a file with the same name and a .json ending. It holds what Google knows about that photo: when it was taken, where, the caption you wrote, who is in it, and whether it was a favourite. The pictures themselves are often exported without that information inside them.

Why they are a problem

  • Most programs ignore them, so your places and captions seem to vanish.
  • There are hundreds or thousands of them, mixed in with your photos.
  • Long names get cut off and duplicates get numbered (1), so a photo and its file can end up with different names.
  • Edited copies, Live Photos and album copies add more of them.

What to do with them

Do not just delete them. If you do, the dates, places and captions are gone for good. First write the information into the photos. Then the .json files have done their job.

  • Photo Clinic matches each photo with its .json file, even when the names were cut or numbered.
  • It writes the dates, places, captions, people and favourites into the photos.
  • It pairs Live Photos back up and matches edited copies to originals.
  • It keeps your Takeout untouched and puts the result in a new folder.
Good to know. Some old video formats (AVI, MKV, WMV, MPG, MTS) and BMP files cannot hold this kind of information, so those only get their file date fixed.
