Damaged photos

How to find damaged or unreadable photos on a Mac

A photo that stopped short, an empty file, or a zip that never finished downloading can cause import errors later. Find them first, and rescue the good files from a failing drive.

Download for Mac

What it finds

  • Pictures and videos that are cut short, with the end missing.
  • Empty files with a name and nothing inside.
  • Files that cannot be opened or are named as one type and are really another.
  • Zip files that are damaged or incomplete.
  • Each one is listed with the reason, in plain words, and left out of a fixed copy.

Check more files, if you want to be thorough

The normal check looks at the structure of every file, which is quick. Check more files (slower) also reads each file all the way through and opens pictures and videos to catch ones that are corrupt on the inside.

Rescue the good files from a failing drive

  • Copies what still reads, gently, so a struggling drive is not pushed harder.
  • Skips a stuck file after a short wait, carries on, and lists what it skipped.
  • Tries the stuck ones again last, and can try again later.
  • Lists a good copy elsewhere when it finds one.

Zip files that did not finish

If a Google Takeout zip stopped early, Photo Clinic says which one, so you can download it again, and still fixes everything else.

How photos get damaged

  • A download or copy that stopped part-way, leaving a file that exists but ends early.
  • A drive that is failing, or was disconnected while it was being written to.
  • A full disk during a copy or import.
  • A zip file that did not finish downloading.
  • Old media that has simply degraded.

Checking one photo by hand

Press the space bar on a photo in Finder to open Quick Look, or open it in Preview. A picture that is cut short usually shows a grey or smeared bottom part, or will not open at all. For a zip file, Terminal can test it: unzip -t file.zip reads every file in it and reports any that are bad. That is fine for one file, and slow for hundreds, which is where a tool helps.

What to do with a damaged file

  • Look for another copy. A backup, your phone, a second drive, or Google Photos may have a good one. Photo Clinic lists a good copy elsewhere when it finds one.
  • Do not delete the damaged file until you have found a replacement. Parts of it can sometimes still be recovered.
  • Download a damaged zip again. The rest of your Takeout can still be fixed in the meantime.
