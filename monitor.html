Monitor

Is Photos syncing, or is it stuck?

A big iCloud library can take days or weeks to sync, and from the outside it is hard to tell working from stuck. Photo Clinic keeps watch, shows the trend, and tells you in plain words.

Download for Mac

A plain verdict

One word and one sentence on what to do. It only reads; it never writes to your library.

HealthyNothing is wrong and nothing is happening.
DownloadingCatching up with iCloud. Normal.
MergingAlbums and edits being merged. Normal; Apple needs days.
Waiting on syncPhoto analysis is waiting for iCloud.
ErrorsiCloud is returning errors.
StuckOnly when every sign agrees (see below).

What it records

Your library

Photos and videos (visible, hidden, deleted), albums and how many have been analysed.

iCloud's sync backlog

The size of the queue iCloud works through. It should shrink over time.

Errors

iCloud error counts, and how busy the sync process is.

Trends

A chart for each number, the change over 24 hours, and a warning if the backlog has not moved for hours.

It never guesses

  • Anything it cannot measure is shown as unknown, not as good or bad.
  • A steady heartbeat in the logs is not treated as progress or as a stall.
  • Stuck needs all three: the merge has run for hours, the backlog has not moved, and the sync process is busy. Then it tells you to leave the library alone and keep the evidence.

Also on watch

Live watch

Messages from Photos and iCloud as they happen, with the real file name, date and albums instead of a long id. You can filter by how serious they are.

Keep sync running

For a holiday. Every 10 minutes it checks whether iCloud is truly stuck, and restarts only that service, gently, at most once every two hours. After three tries that did not help it stops and tells you.

Apple system status

Checks Apple's own status page, so you know if the problem is on Apple's side before you change anything.

Status bar and side panel

Shows the job that is running, how far it has got and how long is left, and keeps the last results one click away.

When it runs

  • While Photo Clinic is open, it takes a reading every 15 minutes. You can leave it in the background.
  • A separate background job is optional, so readings continue while the app is closed.
  • A full dashboard page adds a gap check against iCloud.com, a by-year breakdown and your own to-do list.
One permission. To read the Photos database, macOS needs you to give Photo Clinic Full Disk Access once. If it is missing, Photo Clinic says so, names the program and opens the settings page for you.
