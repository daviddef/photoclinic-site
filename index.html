Get your photos out of the shoebox

Photo Clinic puts back the dates, places and captions that Google Photos strips out of your download. Then it checks Apple Photos and iCloud, and tells you in plain words what is wrong and what to do.

Fix my Google Photos download

Every photo gets its story back

Google keeps dates, places and captions in separate .json files and leaves the photos themselves bare. Photo Clinic reads those files and writes everything back into the photos.

  • Dates, locations, captions, people and favourites restored from Google's own files, across many zips and batches.
  • Time zones done properly. Google stores times in UTC. Photo Clinic writes local time with the right offset, using the photo's location or this Mac's time zone.
  • Missing dates rebuilt from every clue: the file name (IMG_20190704_…, PXL_…, screenshots), neighbouring photo numbers, the folder name and the file's own time, each with a confidence you can review.
  • Places filled in from nearby photos or a GPX track, or guessed from a folder name like Johannesburg. Guesses are labelled as guesses and never replace a real location.
  • Place names written in (city, region, country) from an offline list of about 144,000 towns and cities, with no internet needed.
  • Live Photos re-paired and Google's -edited copies matched to their originals, with a choice to keep both, the edit or the original.
  • Albums kept. Google turns albums into folders full of duplicates. Photo Clinic keeps one copy and writes the albums out as keywords and a list.
  • Google Motion Photos have their video pulled out and saved as a normal MP4.
  • A before-and-after storyboard shows a few of your real photos with what will change in the date, place and caption, before anything runs.

Pick how careful you want to be

Five styles set every option for you, from Safest (only fills gaps, changes nothing it does not have to) to I like risk (maximum clean-up and guessing). Balanced is the recommended mix.

  • Every style starts as a preview. Nothing changes until you untick it and press start.
  • Compare all five in one table and see exactly what each one turns on, including batch sizes and how alike two photos must be to count as a match.
  • Save your own settings as a favourite with the star.
  • Options that can delete, overwrite or guess carry a warning sign.

Check Photos

Is Apple Photos okay? Is it all in iCloud?

Photos and iCloud fail quietly. Photo Clinic reads what they will not tell you and explains it. It only looks; it changes nothing.

  • Check everything for me. One verdict (healthy, needs attention or problem) from four checks: your library files, Photos' upload progress, the Photos and iCloud logs, and this Mac's free space, battery, power mode and heat.
  • Is it all uploaded? Counts what is in Photos and what has reached iCloud, works out your upload speed and time left, and says so when uploads have stopped moving for about 45 minutes.
  • Which photos have not uploaded? Lists every picture still waiting, with its size, date and the likely reason: an empty file, a wrong extension, an unusual type, a huge file or a missing original.
  • Albums and Live Photos arrived? Compares what you sent with what Photos holds.
  • Log issues. Reads recent Photos, iCloud and crash messages and explains each one: a full disk, a full iCloud plan, a dropped network, a sign-in problem, a damaged library, a repair in progress, files Photos refused to import and more.
  • Live watch. Streams the messages as they happen, and shows the real file name, date and albums of the picture a message is about instead of a long id.
  • Sync meter and queue check. Shows live how fast Photos is talking to iCloud, and whether iCloud's own sync queue is shrinking or going round in circles.
  • Where is my disk space going? Shows what takes the space and how much Photos could still free, for when Optimise Mac Storage is on but the library is not shrinking.
  • Look up a photo from an id in a log, or part of a file name.
  • Migration receipt. A page you can share, showing what was done, how complete the metadata is and what arrived in Photos and iCloud.

Photos Health

A reading every 15 minutes

Big iCloud libraries can take days or weeks to sync, and from the outside it is hard to tell “working” from “stuck”. Photos Health records how your library is doing all day and shows the trend, so you do not have to guess. It only reads; it never writes to your library.

HealthyNothing is wrong and nothing is happening.
DownloadingCatching up with iCloud. Normal.
MergingAlbums and edits being merged. Normal; Apple needs days.
Waiting on syncPhoto analysis is waiting for iCloud.
ErrorsiCloud is returning errors.
StuckOnly when every sign agrees: the merge has run for hours, the backlog has not moved, and the sync process is busy.
  • What it records: photos and videos (visible, hidden, deleted), albums, how many photos are analysed and waiting, the size of iCloud's sync backlog, iCloud error counts and how busy the sync process is.
  • Trends, not snapshots: a chart for each number, the change over the last 24 hours, and a warning if the backlog has not moved for hours.
  • Never guesses. Anything it cannot measure is shown as unknown, and it will not call a steady heartbeat in the logs “progress” or “stuck” on its own.
  • Keeps running when the app is closed if you install its small background job. It can tell you when something needs attention, and it keeps your history so you can show Apple the evidence.
  • A full dashboard page with a gap check against iCloud.com, trends, a by-year breakdown and your own to-do list.

Safety

Built so you can trust it with your only copy

  • Preview first, always. Every run starts as a preview that shows exactly what would change.
  • Copy by default. Your originals stay where they are unless you choose otherwise.
  • Stop and undo. Stop any job, and put everything back from a copy or move run with one click.
  • Reports and logs of every run, with a one-tap diagnostic summary if you need help.
  • Faulty drives handled. Copying waits, retries and carries on where it stopped.
  • Private. It runs on your Mac. There is no account, nothing is uploaded and nothing phones home. The app is only reachable from your own computer.
  • Reads your Photos library through a copy. Checks read a copy of Photos' database, never the original. Sending photos to Photos goes through Photos' own import.
Please still back up your photos first. Photo Clinic can move or delete files if you ask it to. It is provided as is, with no warranty, so keep your original Takeout until you have checked the result in Photos and iCloud.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. It is free and open source under the MIT licence. There is no account and no subscription.

Which Macs does it run on?

Apple silicon Macs today. It needs no extra software: the tools it uses are built in. An Intel version is not available yet.

Does it work on Windows or iPhone?

Not yet. Photo Clinic is a Mac app, and the Apple Photos checks only make sense on a Mac.

Will it change my Google Takeout files?

No. By default it copies your photos to a new folder and fixes the copies. Your Takeout stays exactly as it was.

Can it break my Apple Photos library?

The checks only read a copy of the database. The only way it adds to Photos is the “send to Apple Photos” step, which uses Photos' own import, starts with a preview, and has a small 20-photo test. Photos has no undo for imports, so run that test first.

Does it work with iCloud Photos switched on?

Yes, that is what the Check Photos and Photos Health tools are for. Give Apple's servers days, not minutes, before deciding something is permanently stuck.

What does it need permission for?

Full Disk Access so it can read the Photos database for the health checks, and permission to control Photos if you use the send-to-Photos step. macOS asks the first time.

Ready to look at your library?

Download it, point it at your Takeout, and read the preview. That costs you nothing and changes nothing.
