How do I fix Google Takeout metadata on a Mac?

Short answer: the real dates, places and captions are in the .json files beside your photos. Fixing the metadata means writing them back into the photos. You can do that by script or with an app.

Download for Mac

What needs fixing

  • Photos show the day you downloaded them, not the day they were taken.
  • Locations and captions are not inside the pictures.
  • Times are stored in UTC, so they can be hours out.
  • Live Photos arrive as a picture and a separate video.
  • Edited copies and album copies sit beside the originals.
  • Hundreds of .json files clutter the folders.

Two ways to do it

With a script or command-line tool

Free scripts and open-source tools exist. They can work well if you are happy using Terminal, installing the tools they need, and reading their notes. Check that the one you pick handles long and numbered file names, edited copies, Live Photos and time zones, and that it works on a copy, not your only set of photos.

With an app (Photo Clinic)

Photo Clinic is a free Mac app with a preview, so you see what would change before anything does. It copies your photos to a new folder and fixes the copies. It also pairs Live Photos, matches edits, flags wrong dates and can rebuild missing ones. No Terminal needed.

The steps, whichever you choose

  1. Keep your Takeout. Download every zip file. Do not delete the Takeout until you have checked the result.
  2. Work on a copy. Never fix your only copy.
  3. Preview first. Look at what would change for a few real photos.
  4. Fix the dates, places and captions. Write local time, not raw UTC.
  5. Check a sample. Open some fixed photos in Photos or Finder and look at the date and place.
  6. Then tidy. Remove duplicates and edited copies you do not want, and move into Apple Photos in batches.

The full list of what Photo Clinic does is on the Google Takeout page.

Some files cannot hold this information. Old video formats (AVI, MKV, WMV, MPG, MTS) and BMP images cannot store dates and places inside the file, so those only get their file date fixed, whichever tool you use.
